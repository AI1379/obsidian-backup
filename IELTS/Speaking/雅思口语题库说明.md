## 🤖 Assistant

对，**雅思口语可以说存在“一段时间内相对稳定的题池”，但不是官方公布、完全固定的题库。**

 官方并不会提前公开“本季度完整口语题库”，所以你在雅思哥、小红书、培训机构看到的所谓“题库”，本质上主要是根据大量考生的**考场回忆汇总**出来的。IELTS 官方的研究也能看出，考官会按照规定的题目和流程进行提问，并不是现场随便想题。[![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://ielts.org&sz=128)IELTS](https://ielts.org/researchers/our-research/research-reports/topic-development-in-the-ielts-speaking-test?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

 实际备考里，口语通常被大家划成三个周期：

 
- **1–4 月**
- **5–8 月**
- **9–12 月**
 每到 **1 月、5 月、9 月**，会出现比较明显的“换题季”：一些旧题保留，一些退出，同时加入新题。这个规律是长期考生回忆和备考机构观察出来的，并不是 IELTS 官方承诺的固定换题日程。多个 2026 年题库追踪来源仍然观察到了这个规律。[![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://ielts-assistant.com&sz=128)IELTS Assistant+1](https://ielts-assistant.com/search.php?q=2026&utm_source=chatgpt.com)

 所以，你可以把它想象成一个**四个月有效的播放列表**：

 
> 不是说这个四个月只有这几十道题，
>  而是说大家不断回忆之后，会逐渐把当季正在使用的话题范围“摸出来”。

 尤其是 **Part 2**，题库的参考价值非常高。比如某个季度不断有考生遇到：

 
> Describe a person who...
>  Describe a place where...
>  Describe an object that...

 那么后面考生遇到相同或非常类似 Cue Card 的概率确实不低。因此提前准备“当季 Part 2 题库”是很正常、也很有效的备考方法。

 但有一个很重要的区别：

 **题库 ≠ 原题必押中。**

 Part 1 的具体问法可能变化；Part 2 的卡片可能措辞略有不同；而 **Part 3 更灵活**，通常围绕 Part 2 的主题继续深入问社会层面、原因、影响、比较、未来趋势等等。所以死背某一道题的完整答案风险很大。

 还有一个特别巧的时间点：**今天正好是 2026 年 9 月 1 日。**

 也就是说，现在刚好进入传统意义上的 **9–12 月换题季**。目前网上出现的“2026 年 9–12 月口语题库”还属于逐渐补全阶段，因为得等 9 月初一批批考生考完以后，新的题目才会被回忆出来。有追踪 2026 年题目的资料也明确提醒：**9 月第一、第二周题池通常还在逐渐稳定。** [![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://ielts.jp&sz=128)プラスワンポイント](https://ielts.jp/articles/speaking/speaking-topics-2026-september-december/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

 所以如果你**最近要考雅思**，我的建议会是：

 **Part 1：** 看当季题库，但不用每题写答案，练到看到话题就能自然说 20–30 秒。

 **Part 2：重点准备。** 不要准备几十篇独立答案，而是准备大约 **8–12 个“万能故事素材”**。比如一个朋友、一次旅行、一件重要物品、一次成功/失败经历、一个有趣活动，然后让一个素材覆盖好几道题。

 **Part 3：不要背题库答案。** 训练几个思考框架更值，比如：

 **观点 → 原因 → 例子 → 对比/结果**

 这会比背 100 道 Part 3 稳得多。

 如果你是在问：**“那我是不是可以直接照着雅思哥当季口语题库准备？”**

 答案是：**可以，而且口语是四科里最值得参考“当季题库”的一科；只是要拿它当范围，不要拿它当泄题。**

 如果你告诉我你准备**几月份考、目标口语多少分（比如 6 / 6.5 / 7）**，我还可以直接告诉你：题库到底需要准备到什么程度，以及 **Part 2 怎么用十来个故事覆盖几十道题**。

> **记住故事顺序、关键词和五六个稳定句子。**

## 素材一：从化工转向数学与计算科学

 这是你目前**复用率最高**的素材。

 
### 可以覆盖

 
- 🟢 重要决定
- 🟢 改变一个重要决定
- 🟢 对结果满意的决定
- 🟢 改变计划
- 🟢 改变观点
- 🟢 长期理想
- 🟡 完美工作
- 🟡 做了一件困难但成功的事情
 按照你的实际情况，把 `changed my major` 换成 `decided to change my academic direction` 也可以，避免手续状态与事实不一致。

 
### 一分钟纸上写什么

 
```
chemistry → maths
practical but not interested
talked to teachers
extra work / uncertainty
math + coding + engineering
right decision
```

 
### 可直接参考的英文

 
> One important decision I made was to move from chemical engineering towards mathematics and computational science.
> 
>  I originally chose chemical engineering because it seemed practical and offered relatively stable career opportunities. However, after studying at university for some time, I gradually realised that I was much more interested in solving mathematical problems and writing programs.
> 
>  Before making the decision, I spoke to several teachers and classmates, compared the course requirements, and thought seriously about the kind of work I wanted to do in the future. It was quite stressful because changing direction meant extra work and a certain amount of uncertainty. I also needed to catch up on some mathematics courses.
> 
>  Still, I believe it was the right decision. Now I can connect mathematics with programming, AI and engineering projects, which suits me much better. Looking back, the experience changed my opinion about education. I used to think the safest major was automatically the best one, but now I believe genuine interest is equally important.

 
### 换题时只改第一句

 **改变计划：**

 
> My original plan was to continue studying chemical engineering, but I eventually decided to change my academic direction.

 **改变观点：**

 
> I used to believe that choosing a practical major was more important than following my interests, but I later changed my mind.

 **长期理想：**

 
> My long-term ambition is to work at the intersection of mathematics, computing and engineering.

 **令自己满意的重要决定：**

 
> A decision that made me very happy with the result was changing my academic direction.

## 素材二：调试自己设计的 FPGA/Zynq 电路板

 这是你回答**技术问题类**的王牌。它足够真实，而且比“我的手机没电了”有辨识度；但不要把答案说成电子工程答辩，要给普通考官解释明白。

 
### 可以覆盖

 
- 🟢 遇到的技术难题
- 🟢 使用电子设备时遇到的问题
- 🟢 做困难事情并成功
- 🟡 运用想象力解决问题
- 🟡 聪明解决问题的人
- 🟡 想拥有或改进的技术设备
- 🟡 一个应用或程序
 
### 一分钟笔记

 
```
self-designed board
SD errors + no serial output
thought software
used oscilloscope
1.8V vs 3.3V
evidence before guessing
```

 
### 可直接参考的英文

 
> One challenging technological problem I faced happened when I was testing a circuit board that I had designed myself. It used a Zynq chip, which is basically a small computer combined with programmable hardware.
> 
>  During the test, the SD card kept reporting errors, and the serial port produced no useful output. At first, I assumed it was a software problem, so I rebuilt the operating system and checked the drivers several times. However, nothing changed.
> 
>  I then stopped changing the software randomly and began measuring the electrical signals with an oscilloscope. Eventually, I discovered that one group of pins was operating at 1.8 volts, while the connected devices expected 3.3 volts. That small voltage mismatch explained both problems.
> 
>  I located the cause and worked out how to correct it in the next version of the board. Although the process was frustrating, it was also extremely satisfying. It taught me to collect evidence first instead of immediately blaming the software.

 
### 这里容易说错的词

 
- oscilloscope：可以直接说
   **a device used to measure electrical signals**
- programmable hardware：不要再继续解释 FPGA 架构
- voltage mismatch：比 `the voltage was wrong` 更自然，但也不难
- rebuild the operating system：比展开讲 PetaLinux、驱动树、FSBL 更适合考场

## 素材三：三个人一起做数学项目，最后主动缩小范围

 这条素材可以很好地表现你有判断力，而不仅是“我们分工合作，最后成功了”的模板故事。

 
### 可以覆盖

 
- 🟢 一次小组合作
- 🟢 改变计划
- 🟢 改变观点
- 🟢 做困难事情并成功
- 🟡 给别人建议
- 🟡 擅长规划的人
- 🟡 聪明解决问题的人
- 🟡 鼓励别人的经历
 
### 一分钟笔记

 
```
3 students
maths / physics / number theory
original idea too ambitious
too many forced connections
suggested simplifying
clearer presentation
```

 
### 可直接参考的英文

 
> One time I worked in a group was for a university mathematics project. There were three of us, and we had quite different academic interests. I focused more on computation, one teammate studied physics, and another was particularly interested in number theory.
> 
>  At first, our plan was far too ambitious. We tried to connect conformal maps, string theory, number theory and neural networks in a single presentation. It sounded impressive, but the connections were not always clear, and the audience would probably have found it difficult to follow.
> 
>  I suggested removing the neural-network section because it felt slightly forced. Instead, we decided to begin with a concrete mathematical example and then introduce the deeper theory. We divided the remaining work according to our strengths and met several times to combine the slides.
> 
>  In the end, the presentation became much clearer. This experience taught me that successful teamwork does not mean including every idea. Sometimes the most intelligent decision is to simplify the project.

 这段中最有价值的一句是：

 
> Sometimes the most intelligent decision is to simplify the project.

 它可以放到很多“建议、决定、合作、困难”题的结尾。

## 素材四：一门让你印象深刻的计算数学课程

 “印象深刻的课程”已经在 8 月 31 日的九月题库动态页面中出现，所以今晚应该单独说熟。

 课程名换成你**真正上过的课程**：计算数学、数值分析、程序设计、高等数学都可以。

 
### 可以覆盖

 
- 🟢 印象深刻的课程
- 🟢 改变观点
- 🟢 长期理想
- 🟡 完美工作
- 🟡 学到的重要技能
 
### 一分钟笔记

 
```
computational maths
equations → algorithms
write code
accuracy and stability
math + programming connected
influenced future direction
```

 
### 可直接参考的英文

 
> A course that impressed me a lot was a computational mathematics course at university. What made it special was that it showed me how abstract mathematical equations could be turned into practical algorithms.
> 
>  In a typical class, we first learned a mathematical method and then implemented it in code. After that, we tested how accurate and stable the program was. Before taking the course, I thought mathematics and programming were almost separate subjects. Mathematics seemed theoretical, while programming seemed mainly practical. The course completely changed that view.
> 
>  I found it fascinating because it provided a bridge between theory and real engineering problems. It also influenced my decision to study mathematics and computation more seriously. Although some assignments were difficult and took a lot of time, they gave me a strong sense of achievement.

 Part 3 要是问“大学课程应该偏理论还是实践”，你直接接：

 
> I think students need both. Theory tells us why a method works, while practical projects show whether we can actually use it.

## 素材五：钢琴和绝对音感

 这是你的人物、能力、音乐类备用素材，也能在抽到比较轻松的题时让你迅速进入状态。

- 🟡 一位有创造力的人

### 可直接参考的英文

 
> One ability I would like to improve is my musical ear and my piano playing. I have absolute pitch, which means I can usually identify a musical note without hearing another reference note first.

> I see it as a starting point rather than an achievement by itself.

- 🟡 安静的地方

- 🟡 喜欢去的地方

# 四、人物题不要准备六个人，准备“两人一模板”

 人物题最容易把人吓住，因为表面上有：

 
- 儿时朋友
- 快乐的人
- 擅长语言的人
- 克服困难的人
- 成功经营生意的人
- 喜欢种植物的人
- 从事医疗工作的人
- 只见过一次但想继续了解的人
 实际上你只需准备：

 
### 人物 A：一位真实同学或队友

 可以覆盖：

 
- 做困难事情并成功
- 擅长规划
- 聪明解决问题
- 乐于助人
- 擅长语言
- 快乐的人
 
### 人物 B：一位亲戚、老师或只见过一次的讲座嘉宾

 可以覆盖：

 
- 医疗工作者
- 经营生意的人
- 种植物的人
- 想继续了解的人
- 本地名人
 
## 万能人物骨架

 
> I’d like to talk about **[person]**, who is **[your relationship]**.
>  I first met him/her **[when and where]**.
>  What stands out about this person is **[one clear quality]**.
>  For example, **[one specific story]**.
>  This showed me that he/she is **[quality]**, rather than simply **[surface description]**.
>  That is why I admire him/her and would like to know more about him/her.

 关键不是介绍他的年龄、身高、家乡，而是一定要有：

 
> **一个性格特点 + 一件具体小事**

 例如“擅长语言”不要只说：

 
> She speaks English very well.

 要说：

 
> During a group discussion, she could explain the same technical idea in both simple English and Chinese, so everyone understood it immediately.

# 五、几个冷门题的应急选材

 这些不用写完整答案，今晚把故事决定下来即可。

 
## 🟢 想制定的新法律／环境保护法律

 你最适合讲：

 
### 电子废弃物回收制度

 
> I would introduce a law requiring electronics manufacturers and large retailers to collect used batteries and electronic devices for proper recycling.

 理由说三点：

# 七、明天 Part 2 的一分钟草稿怎么写

 官方 Part 2 是**一分钟准备、连续说一到两分钟**，可以在准备时间做笔记。目标不是把提示点逐条机械回答，而是形成一个连贯故事，尽量接近完整的两分钟。[![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://ielts.org&sz=128)IELTS+1](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-sample-tests/ielts-speaking-sample-tasks-2023.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

 你拿到纸后，只写六格：

 
```
Who / What
Background
Problem
Action
Result
Feeling / lesson
```

 比如“技术难题”：

 
```
my circuit board
SD + serial failed
thought software
measured signals
1.8V / 3.3V
evidence first
```

 **不要写完整句子。**写完整句子的结果通常是：第一段照着说得很顺，纸上的内容用完以后立刻停住。

 
## 你应该固定的连接句

 开场：

 
> The first example that comes to mind is…

 背景：

 
> To give you some background,…

 引出困难：

 
> The main difficulty was that…

 补充例子：

 
> To give you a specific example,…

 回顾：

 
> Looking back, I think…

 结尾：

 
> So overall, it was a challenging but valuable experience.

# 八、今晚的实际练法

 不要再阅读半小时题库却一题都不开口。按这个顺序：

 
1. **专业转向**：分别以“决定、改变计划、改变观点、长期理想”四种开头说一遍。

>  **具体小事，比空泛大道理更容易说满两分钟。**
>  **忘掉一个词就换说法，不要停在那里搜词。**

 One technological problem I once faced was when I was developing an AI agent for programming tasks.

The basic idea was to let the AI read a software project, understand the user's request, and then modify the code automatically. At first, I thought the difficult part would be choosing a good language model. However, I soon realised that the bigger problem was how to make the agent use tools correctly and keep track of what it had already done.

For example, sometimes the agent would search the same file repeatedly, or make a change without checking whether the program still worked. In other cases, it would misunderstand the structure of the project and edit the wrong part of the code.

To solve this, I changed the workflow. I asked the agent to make a plan before editing anything, keep its steps relatively small, and run tests after important changes. I also tried to separate different tasks, such as searching, reasoning and actually modifying files.

After several experiments, the system became much more reliable. It still wasn't perfect, but the experience taught me something important: building an AI agent is not simply about using the most powerful model. The way you organise the whole process can be just as important as the model itself.

- **AI agent for programming tasks**：用于编程任务的 AI Agent
- **read a software project**：读取/理解一个软件项目
- **modify the code automatically**：自动修改代码
- **use tools correctly**：正确调用工具
- **keep track of what it had already done**：记住/追踪自己已经做过什么
- **the structure of the project**：项目结构
- **edit the wrong part of the code**：改错代码的位置

- **run tests after important changes**
- **the whole workflow / process**：整个工作流
 这里尤其要注意：IELTS 里你**完全不需要**说什么 MCP、tool calling、context window、sub-agent、OpenAI-compatible provider 这种东西。你知道得越深，反而越容易忍不住解释，然后把 Part 2 变成技术报告。

## 如果题目是 “Describe a difficult task you completed”

 主体几乎一字不用改，只换开头：

 A difficult task I once completed was building a simple AI agent that could help with programming.

What made it difficult was that I wasn't simply writing a normal program with a fixed sequence of steps. The AI had to make decisions by itself, such as which files to read, what information was relevant and what changes it should make.

I initially thought that using a stronger AI model would solve the problem, but I gradually realised that the design of the workflow was equally important.

It took me quite a lot of experimentation, but eventually the system became much more reliable. I found the project challenging but also very interesting, because it combined programming, problem-solving and AI.

> **The AI had to make decisions by itself.**

# 如果你想讲“算法 / 计算数学”，我更推荐这个故事

 你可以讲：**第一次真正理解梯度下降、Momentum、Adam 这一类优化算法，不再只会套公式。**

 这个比 Agent 更学术，也可以用在：

 
- difficult subject
- something difficult you learned
- an interesting course
- a useful skill
- something you learned that surprised you
- a difficult problem
 One difficult topic I learned was optimisation algorithms used in machine learning.

When I first studied neural networks, I knew that algorithms such as gradient descent and Adam were used to train them, but my understanding was quite superficial. I could use the formulas, but I didn't really understand why different optimisation methods behaved differently.

So I decided to study the mathematics behind them more carefully. I compared ordinary gradient descent with methods such as momentum and Adam, and I wrote some small programs to visualise how they moved towards the minimum of a function.

The visualisation helped me a lot. Instead of just looking at equations, I could actually see why momentum sometimes allowed an algorithm to move faster, or why a poor choice of parameters could make the optimisation unstable.

It took some time because I had to connect mathematical ideas with programming and machine learning. But once I understood it, I found it extremely satisfying. It also made me realise that computational mathematics is interesting precisely because abstract mathematics can produce very practical algorithms.

 其中：

 
### 梯度下降

 你明天可以说：

 
> **gradient descent, which is an algorithm for finding a better solution step by step**

 不要解释偏导数。

 
### 最小值

 
> **the minimum of a function**

 这个词你数学背景下肯定不难，但如果现场突然忘了：

 
> **the best point**

 也能救。

 
### 优化算法

 
> **optimisation algorithm**

 英式 IELTS 拼 `optimisation`，但口语当然听不出 s/z。

 
### 收敛

 尽量别主动说 `convergence`。

 直接说：

 
> **move towards the solution**

 就够了。

 “从原理图做到实际能工作的板子”：

 
> **turn a design on paper into a working device**

 这句特别棒，而且没有任何技术门槛。

 
---
 
# 如果是“做困难的事情并成功的人”

 假如符合他真实经历，你可以用**他自己设计 FPGA 板并最终调通**作为具体事件。

 不用讲供电、DDR、JTAG 那一大堆。

 
> One person I know who managed to do something quite difficult is a close friend of mine who is really interested in electronics.
> 
>  He often works on FPGA projects. An FPGA is basically a programmable chip that can be used to build specialised digital systems. Some time ago, he decided to design his own circuit board around one of these chips.
> 
>  What made the project difficult was that there were many things that could go wrong. He had to design the hardware, choose suitable components, assemble the board and then debug it. Even a very small mistake could stop the whole system from working.
> 
>  I remember that when he first tested the board, it didn't work as expected. Instead of giving up or immediately starting again, he checked the problem step by step. He measured different signals, changed parts of the design and gradually eliminated possible causes.
> 
>  Eventually, he managed to get the system working. What impressed me wasn't simply the final result. It was his patience. I'm much more interested in algorithms and software myself, so watching him solve a physical engineering problem was quite different from the kind of work I usually do.
> 
>  I think that's why I remember the experience so clearly. It showed me that difficult problems often require patience more than one brilliant idea.

 这一段结尾特别适合你：

 
> **Difficult problems often require patience more than one brilliant idea.**

 
---
 
## 而且你和他的专业差异是一个很好用的细节

 这会让答案瞬间从模板作文变成真实的人际关系：

 
> **I'm more interested in algorithms and computational mathematics, while he's much more into electronics and hardware.**

 然后：

 
> **So we sometimes look at the same technical problem from completely different perspectives.**

 非常自然。

 甚至可以继续：

 
> **I tend to think about software or algorithms first, while he usually thinks about what is happening at the hardware level.**

 这比：

 
> He is my friend and he is very smart.

 不知道强到哪里去了。

# 如果题目是“一个聪明的人”

 还是人物 A。

 开头换成：

 
> A person I consider particularly intelligent is a close friend of mine who is very good at hardware design.

 然后有一个很重要的 IELTS 技巧：

 **不要把 smart 定义成“考试成绩很好”。**

 你可以说：

 
> What makes me think he is intelligent is not that he knows every answer immediately. It's that he is very systematic when he doesn't know the answer.

 这个句子特别漂亮，而且很好说。

 继续：

 
> When something goes wrong, he doesn't just try random solutions. He breaks the problem into smaller parts and checks them one by one.

 这已经足够证明“聪明”。

# “快乐的人”不要强套

 这个我要特别提醒你。

 如果这个朋友本人确实比较外向、乐观、经常很开心，那么当然可以套。

 否则不要为了实现“一个人物打天下”，突然：

 
> He is also the happiest person I know.

 考官：？

 😂

 人物题串题不是要求一个人承担宇宙中所有人格。

 所以你的 A/B 体系我会这样划：

 
| 题型 | 首选 |
| --- | --- |
| 只见过一次、想进一步了解 | **B Okounkov ★★★★★** |
| 著名/成功的人 | **B Okounkov ★★★★☆** |
| 敬佩的人 | **B Okounkov / A 都行** |
| 聪明的人 | **A 朋友 ★★★★★** |
| 做困难事情成功的人 | **A 朋友 ★★★★★** |
| 擅长解决问题的人 | **A 朋友 ★★★★★** |
| 不同兴趣的朋友 | **A 朋友 ★★★★★** |
| 一起学习/讨论的人 | **A 朋友** |
| 快乐的人 | 另找真实人物 |
| 擅长语言的人 | 另找真实人物 |
| 医疗工作者 | 另找 |
| 喜欢种植物的人 | 另找 |
| 儿时朋友 | 如果 A 确实是，否则另找 |

 所以实际上不需要准备十个人。

 你的“大人物池”就是：

 
> **A = 一个我很熟，因此能讲具体故事的人**
>  **B = 一个我不熟，但一次 encounter 本身就很有故事的人**

 这恰恰是最稳的组合。

## Okounkov 还有几个非常好用的英文表达

 你明天如果抽到他，以下词组够用了：

 
- **a Fields Medalist**
- **a well-known mathematician**
- **an academic lecture**
- **advanced mathematics**
- **different branches of mathematics**
- **connect apparently unrelated ideas**
- **approach a mathematical problem**
- **his way of thinking**
- **beyond my current level**
- **I couldn't follow every technical detail**
- **what impressed me most was...**
 甚至 Fields Medal 都不用解释复杂：

 
> **The Fields Medal is one of the most prestigious awards in mathematics.**

 就结束。

 不要说 `the Nobel Prize of mathematics`。日常比喻有人这么讲，但考试里你既然自己是数学方向，完全可以说得更准确一点。

 最后，如果考官突然追问：

 
> Why do you want to know more about him?

 你千万别现场搜索什么获奖履历，直接：

 
> **Because I want to know how a mathematician at that level actually thinks when facing a problem he has never seen before.**

 这句我觉得非常适合你，而且它把 Okounkov 从“一个我其实不了解的名人”彻底变成了一个**与你自己的数学兴趣有关的人物故事**。[![](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.mathunion.org&sz=128)国际数学联盟](https://www.mathunion.org/imu-awards/fields-medal/fields-medals-2006?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
